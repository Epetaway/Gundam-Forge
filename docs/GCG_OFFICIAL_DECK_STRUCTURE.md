# Official Gundam TCG Deck Structure

## Overview

The **Official Gundam Combined Card Game (GCG)** requires a strictly structured deck: **exactly 50 cards in the main deck** and up to 10 cards in the resource deck. An optional side deck can be used for tournament play.

---

## Deck Composition

### Deck Size Limits (Strictly Enforced)
**Main Deck**: exactly 50 cards (no more, no less)
**Resource Deck**: up to 10 cards (optional, 0-10 cards)
**Optional Side Deck**: 0-15 cards (tournament use only)

### Deck Split

| Component | Cards | Purpose |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Main Deck** | Exactly 50 cards | Primary gameplay deck (draws, plays cards, attacks) |

---

## Understanding the Split

### What's the Difference?

**Main Deck (Exactly 50 cards)**
- You draw cards from this during the Draw Phase
- You play units, pilots, and commands from your hand (drawn from main deck)
- This is what you think of as your "hand and play deck"
- When empty, you lose the game
- Minimum 1 card required to play

**Resource Deck (up to 10 cards)**
- Separate optional deck created at game start
- **You NEVER draw from this manually**
- During the Resource Phase, you automatically place the top card of this deck into your resource area
- Its sole purpose is to feed the Resource Phase (1 card per turn max)
- When empty, that phase is simply skipped
- You can have 0 resource cards (Resource Phase will be skipped all game)

**Side Deck (0-15 cards) — Optional**
- Only used in tournament play
- Between games of a match, swap cards from your main/resource deck with cards from your side deck to tune your strategy
- Each card can have up to 4 copies (independent of main deck counts)
- Cannot modify your main deck + resource deck total when swapping
- Used to adapt to your opponent's strategy

## Deck Construction Rules (Official GCG 2025)

### Size Requirements
- **Main Deck**: Exactly 50 cards (required)
- **Resource Deck**: 0-10 cards maximum (optional)
- **Can build incrementally** — start with 1 card and build up to 50
- Exactly 50 cards in main deck required to play a game (deck must be full)

### Card Limits
- **Maximum 4 copies** of any single card (official GCG limit)
- Some cards may have lower limits (check card text)
- **Colorless cards** don't count toward color restrictions
- **Side deck cards** have independent 4-copy limit

### Playtester Display

When you see your deck count in the playtester:
- **Main Deck: 40 cards** — Cards you'll draw from (example)
- **Resource Deck: 10 cards** — Cards that feed Resource Phase (example)
- **Total: 50 cards** — Your playable deck (example)

**Deck Building Examples:**
- Minimal deck: 1 main + 0 resource = 1 card (can play with this)
- Standard deck: 45 main + 8 resource = 53 cards (fully playable)
- Optimized deck: 50 main + 10 resource = 60 cards (maximum)

## Game Setup (Rule 6 - Initialization)

When a game starts with a constructed deck (example: 50 main + 10 resource):

1. Both main deck and resource deck are shuffled separately
2. Player 1 draws starting hand of 5 cards from main deck
3. Top 6 cards of main deck are placed as shields (face-down)
4. Remaining cards stay in main deck
5. Resource deck sits aside, ready for Resource Phase

### Shield Count & Remaining Deck
- **6 shields** placed from the top of the main deck (official GCG rule)
- **5 cards** drawn for opening hand
- **Math check** (example): 50 main - 6 shields - 5 hand = **39 cards remaining** ✓

## Resource Phase Flow (Rule 4-4)

Each turn during the Resource Phase:

1. **Check resource deck**: Is there a card on top?
   - **Yes**: Move it to your resource area (enters as "ready"/active)
   - **No**: Resource Phase is skipped this turn
2. **Resource generated**: You can now spend this resource for card costs
```3. **Once per turn**: Only 1 resource can be placed per Resource Phase

Since the resource deck is 10 cards and a standard game lasts ~8-10 turns, you should have resources available for most of your game.

## Common Questions

### Q: Why have a separate resource deck?
**A:** Official GCG rules ensure resources are always fairly distributed. Instead of hoping to draw resource cards, every player gets exactly 10 resources to use throughout the game.

### Q: What if my resource deck runs out?
**A:** The Resource Phase is simply skipped that turn. You don't lose or draw—it's just not available. This rarely matters since games typically end before resources are exhausted.

### Q: How do I build my deck correctly?
**A:** When you create a deck:
- Include your **main deck cards** (up to 50)
- Include your **resource cards** (up to 10)
- You can build incrementally — start with 1 card if you want
- No requirement for exact counts; build to whatever limit works for your strategy

### Q: What's the proper deck count?
- **Main deck**: 1-50 cards maximum
- **Resource deck**: 0-10 cards maximum (optional)
- **Minimum to play**: 1 card in main deck
- **Maximum total**: 50 main + 10 resource = 60 cards possible

### Q: Can I play with less than 50 cards?
**A:** Yes! You can play with any number from 1 card up to 50 cards in your main deck. Fewer cards may make your deck faster but more predictable. More cards make it more varied but slightly slower.

### Q: Do I need a resource deck?
**A:** No, the resource deck is optional. If you don't include any resource cards, the Resource Phase will simply be skipped every turn. Many casual decks don't use a resource deck.

### Q: What is a side deck?
**A:** A side deck is an optional 0-15 card additional set for tournament play. Between games of a match, you can swap any cards from your main/resource deck with cards from your side deck to tune your strategy. Your total deck must still be 60 cards (50 main + 10 resource) after swaps.

### Q: How do side deck copies work?
**A:** The 4-copy limit per card applies independently to main deck and side deck. Example:
- Main deck: 4 copies of "Card X"
- Side deck: 4 additional copies of "Card X"
- Total possible: 8 copies across both decks, but only 4 in main at any time

### Q: Can I use my side deck for casual play?
**A:** Side decks are primarily for tournament format. Casual or single-game play doesn't use a side deck. If using one, make sure to swap cards back to your original configuration before the next opponent.
